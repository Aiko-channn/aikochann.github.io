Chapter Nine. And so the evening settled over the house like water, and every window held a small yellow lamp, and she thought of him in the way one thinks of weather — not as a thing that happens but as a climate one lives inside. There had been letters before this one, letters folded into books and letters burned in sinks, but this letter was different because it told no news whatsoever. It said only what had always been true and had never once required proof: that love is not a grand event but a thousand ordinary ones, repeated until they become a life. Outside, the last light went violet, then gray, then to stars, and the house kept its small lamps burning, and he read her words twice, and understood them differently each time.

My Dearest,

I love you. It is a simple sentence, and I mean it simply.

I love how easy it is to be with you. We can do nothing at all and it still feels like enough. A quiet evening, a short walk, a bad movie. You make ordinary days matter.

I love your laugh. It fills the room, and I will do almost anything to hear it.

I love that you see me. You notice when I am tired before I say it. You know when my smile is real. With you, I do not have to pretend.

I love how you handle the hard days. We do not always agree, but you listen, you try, and you care more about us than about being right. I try to do the same.

You have made me kinder and more patient. You have made me want to be better.

I do not need a big picture of the future. I just know you are in every version of it I imagine. Ordinary days, with you.

So this is what I mean when I say I love you. You are my home, my favorite person, and my first choice. Every day.

Thank you for being you.

With all my love,
Forever Yours
